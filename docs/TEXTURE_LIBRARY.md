# World texture library: first batch (for review)

What the chat model gets when the recent messages mention one of these: the summary, the tired version to avoid, and 3 of the specifics, picked at random each time (at most 2 entries per reply). Bulba can read whole entries when writing cards, personas and lore.

Edit the source file `narrative/mainapp/data/texture/library.json`, or tell me what to change. 🔞 = only when mature content is on.

## Luxury

### Old money
**Wakes on:** old money, aristocra, heir, heiress, estate, manor, inheritance, аристократ, спадко, маєток

Old wealth is quiet and slightly worn: things are inherited, repaired, used for decades. Status shows in access and ease (who they can call, what they never have to ask), not in labels.

**Avoid:** logos, gold everywhere, talk about prices, a butler named Jeeves

**Specifics:**
- a cashmere sweater with a darned elbow
- a signet ring worn smooth
- a car twenty years old and perfectly kept
- an umbrella stand of walking sticks nobody uses
- a family portrait hung slightly crooked and never fixed
- a club membership that comes with a waiting list of decades
- thank-you letters on engraved card
- a cold house because heating the east wing is 'absurd'
- summers at the same place since childhood
- knowing which fork without thinking, and not caring who doesn't
- a dog more important than most guests
- a tailor who has their measurements since school

### New money and status display
**Wakes on:** new money, billionaire, millionaire, tycoon, yacht, penthouse, мільйонер, мільярдер, яхт, пентхаус

New wealth is visible and time-starved: assistants, schedules, things bought for the experience or the photo. The anxiety is about access and being taken seriously by older money.

**Avoid:** the word 'expensive' in every line, champagne in every scene, a character who only talks about money

**Specifics:**
- a phone that never stops buzzing, answered by an assistant
- a watch on a waitlist bought through a 'friend of the dealer'
- a chef hired for one dinner
- a penthouse with almost no furniture and perfect lighting
- a driver who also handles the security briefing
- art bought on an advisor's word
- a private jet that is really a share in a fleet
- a membership to a members-only club in three cities
- the same sneakers in six colours
- a wellness routine with a cold plunge at 5 a.m.
- renting the whole restaurant rather than waiting for a table

### Jewellery and fine objects
**Wakes on:** jewel, necklace, diamond, pearl, earring, bracelet, brooch, коштовн, намист, діамант, перл, сережк, брошк

Fine jewellery has weight, temperature and history: clasps, settings, repairs, who wore it before. Describe one specific piece by how it behaves, not by its value.

**Avoid:** 'glittering diamonds', 'priceless necklace', jewels described only by price

**Specifics:**
- a pearl strand that has to be restrung every few years
- a brooch pinned on the left because 'that is where it goes'
- earrings heavy enough to leave a mark by evening
- a ring resized twice for different hands
- an Art Deco clasp that needs two hands
- a locket with a photo nobody remembers
- a chain warm from being worn all day
- a stone with a small inclusion only the owner knows is there
- a pair of cufflinks borrowed and never returned
- a tiara kept in a bank vault, worn once a decade

## Clothing

### Regency and early 19th-century dress
**Wakes on:** regency, bridgerton, empire waist, 1810, ball gown, регентств, бальн

Regency clothing is light muslin for women, layered tailoring for men; dressing takes help and time, and clothes signal rank, mourning, marriage status.

**Avoid:** corsets cinched to fainting, everyone in silk ballgowns all day

**Specifics:**
- a high-waisted muslin dress that shows every grass stain
- a spencer jacket against the evening chill
- kid gloves removed only to eat
- a reticule too small for anything but a fan and a card
- a cravat tied in a style with its own name
- Hessian boots polished by someone else
- a shawl that is half the point of the outfit
- half-mourning grey and lavender
- a bonnet brim that hides the face from the side
- ink-stained cuffs on a man who writes his own letters

### Victorian dress
**Wakes on:** victorian, 1880, bustle, crinoline, corset, вікторіан, корсет, кринолін

Victorian clothes are heavy, layered and rule-bound: different outfits for morning, visiting, dinner and mourning; dark wool and soot in the city.

**Avoid:** every woman in a huge ballgown, corsets as torture devices in every scene

**Specifics:**
- a bustle that makes sitting a negotiation
- jet beads for mourning
- a high collar with a cameo at the throat
- mud-stained hems from city streets
- a gentleman's watch chain across the waistcoat
- detachable celluloid collars
- a walking dress for the park, different from the visiting dress
- button boots needing a buttonhook
- a governess in dark grey with no ornament
- gloves for every occasion, a different length for each

### 1920s fashion
**Wakes on:** 1920, flapper, jazz age, gatsby, art deco, двадцят

The 1920s cut hair and hems: straight dropped-waist dresses, bobbed hair, cloche hats; men in wider trousers and boaters in summer. Movement and dancing shaped the clothes.

**Avoid:** every scene a Gatsby party with champagne towers and feathers

**Specifics:**
- a beaded dress heavy enough to swing when she turns
- a cloche hat pulled down to the eyebrows
- rolled stockings and rouged knees
- a long string of pearls knotted at the chest
- two-tone spectator shoes
- a raccoon coat at a college game
- Oxford bags, trousers wide as skirts
- a cigarette holder that is mostly a prop
- a dropped waist that makes curves irrelevant
- a fresh bob that still feels strange on the neck

### Streetwear and contemporary casual
**Wakes on:** streetwear, hoodie, sneaker, trainers, oversized, кросівк, худі, оверсайз

Contemporary casual is about fit, brand signals and small choices: how the hoodie hangs, which sneakers, what is clean and what is deliberately worn.

**Avoid:** 'casual jeans and a t-shirt' for everyone, or every character in designer labels

**Specifics:**
- sneakers kept in their box between wears
- a hoodie two sizes up, sleeves over the hands

- a crossbody bag worn at the chest

- a puffer jacket on in a warm café
- a silver chain that keeps catching on the collar

- a cap worn backwards only indoors

### Goth and alternative fashion
**Wakes on:** goth, gothic, punk, emo, alt, grunge, гот, панк, ґранж

Alternative fashion is a craft and a community: DIY, layered black textures, deliberate choices, and a history (post-punk, trad goth, emo, grunge) people care about.

**Avoid:** goths as sad and edgy, 'dressed in black' as the whole description

**Specifics:**
- a jacket covered in hand-sewn patches
- platform boots that add four inches and take ten minutes to lace
- fishnets under ripped jeans
- silver rings on every finger, one from a grandmother
- black lipstick worn off on a coffee cup
- a velvet coat from a charity shop
- safety pins holding a hem that tore years ago
- eyeliner that took twenty minutes and looks effortless
- a band shirt from an actual gig in 2009
- studded belt, one stud missing

### Office and corporate dress
**Wakes on:** suit, office, corporate, business casual, boardroom, костюм, офіс, корпоратив

Office clothes signal rank quietly: fit, fabric, what you are allowed to skip. The higher up, the more casual you are permitted to be.

**Avoid:** 'a sharp suit' for every powerful man, a pencil skirt for every woman

**Specifics:**
- a lanyard tucked into the shirt pocket
- a blazer left on the chair to look present
- trainers under the desk, heels in the bag
- a quarter-zip fleece with a company logo
- a tie loosened after six
- a suit that fits at the shoulders and nowhere else
- a coffee stain hidden under a buttoned jacket
- reading glasses pushed into the hair
- a CEO in a plain grey t-shirt that costs more than the intern's suit
- a security badge that has to be tapped twice

### Military and service uniforms
**Wakes on:** uniform, military, officer, soldier, army, navy, уніформ, військов, офіцер, солдат

Uniforms carry rules: rank insignia, how they are worn, what is out of regulation. Field and dress uniforms are different worlds.

**Avoid:** uniforms that are always crisp, medals clanking in every scene

**Specifics:**

## Food

### Ukrainian food
**Wakes on:** borscht, varenyky, ukrainian food, борщ, вареник, сало, голубц, пампушк, деруни, узвар

Ukrainian home food is seasonal, generous and regional: soups, dough, preserves, the dacha harvest. Who cooked it and whose recipe matters more than the dish.

**Avoid:** borscht in every scene, 'hearty peasant food'

**Specifics:**
- green borscht with sorrel and a halved egg
- varenyky with sour cherries and sugar in summer
- garlic pampushky still warm
- deruny with sour cream at a dacha
- a jar of grandmother's pickles opened for guests
- uzvar of dried fruit at Christmas Eve
- kutia with poppy seed and honey
- banosh with brynza in the Carpathians
- salo sliced thin on black bread with garlic
- holubtsi rolled the night before
- kvas from a street barrel in summer
- syrnyky for breakfast, slightly burnt on one side

### Japanese food
**Wakes on:** japanese food, sushi, ramen, izakaya, bento, onigiri, суші, рамен, японськ

Japanese food culture runs from convenience-store onigiri to izakaya to formal kaiseki; seasonality and small rituals (oshibori, itadakimasu) matter.

**Avoid:** sushi as the only Japanese food

**Specifics:**
- convenience-store onigiri with the clever wrapper
- a late-night ramen counter with a ticket machine
- an oshibori towel steaming on arrival
- yakitori and cheap beer at an izakaya
- a bento with a carrot cut like a flower
- natto that divides the table
- a kaiseki course too beautiful to start
- takoyaki burning the roof of the mouth
- a can of hot coffee from a vending machine in winter
- matcha whisked badly by a beginner

### Italian food
**Wakes on:** italian, pasta, trattoria, risotto, espresso, італійськ, паст, різот

Italian food is regional and rule-bound: no cappuccino after a meal, the right pasta shape for the sauce, Sunday lunches that last hours.

**Avoid:** spaghetti and meatballs, everyone shouting 'mamma mia'

**Specifics:**
- an espresso drunk standing at the bar in two sips
- cacio e pepe that seizes if the water is wrong
- a nonna's ragù that takes all Sunday
- aperitivo with olives and crisps
- a trattoria with no menu, only what the owner decided
- fresh pasta drying on a broom handle
- a bitter amaro after dinner
- a risotto stirred for exactly as long as it takes
- a cappuccino ordered at 4 p.m. and the waiter's look
- panettone arguments at Christmas

French food culture is ritual and time: the morning baguette run, the long lunch, the apéro, the cheese course; bistros, brasseries and patisseries are different places.

## Science

### Laboratory work
**Wakes on:** laboratory, lab, experiment, pipette, centrifuge, scientist, researcher, лаборатор, експеримент, науков, дослідник

Real lab work is slow, procedural and full of waiting: protocols, incubations, contamination, failed runs, booking equipment, and a lab notebook that has to be readable.

**Avoid:** 'the experiment was a success' after one try; every scientist a genius with a cold voice

**Specifics:**
- a centrifuge that walks across the bench when unbalanced
- gloves changed every time the phone rings
- a protocol annotated in three people's handwriting
- a -80 freezer alarm at 3 a.m.
- an overnight incubation that dictates the whole weekend
- a contaminated plate and a week lost
- booking the confocal microscope a month ahead
- a fume hood sash pulled down to the line
- coffee forbidden in the lab and drunk in the corridor
- labels written on tape with a marker that's dying
- a reviewer's comment quoted bitterly at lunch

### Hospitals and medicine
**Wakes on:** hospital, doctor, nurse, surgery, surgeon, ER, ward, лікарн, лікар, медсест, хірург, палат

Hospital work is shifts, handovers and hierarchy; nurses run the ward, residents are exhausted, and most of the job is documentation, not heroics.

**Avoid:** a surgeon shouting 'clear!' over every patient, doctors with nothing to do but brood

**Specifics:**
- a handover at 7 a.m. with cold coffee
- a pager going off mid-sentence
- a nurse who knows the patient better than the consultant
- scrubs that never quite fit
- a resident asleep sitting up in the staff room
- a whiteboard of beds in three colours of marker
- hand gel on every doorframe
- a family waiting in the corridor for the same news for hours
- a consultant's ward round with students trailing
- notes typed with one finger at the end of a 12-hour shift

### Astronomy and observatories
**Wakes on:** telescope, observatory, astronom, stars, planet, galaxy, телескоп, обсерватор, астроном, галакти

Modern astronomy is mostly data, code and night shifts at remote sites; observing time is competed for, and weather decides everything.

**Avoid:** a lone genius peering through a brass telescope every night

**Specifics:**
- a night of observing time lost to clouds
- a control room warmer than the dome
- a red flashlight to keep night vision
- data reduction that takes months
- the altitude headache at a mountain observatory
- a telescope proposal rejected by one point
- a light-pollution map on the wall
- an amateur who spots the comet first

## Politics

### Royal courts and nobility
**Wakes on:** king, queen, prince, princess, court, throne, royal, король, королев, принц, трон, двір

Court life is etiquette, access and patronage: who stands where, who is received, who has the monarch's ear; power moves through favours, marriages and offices more than swords.

**Avoid:** a cartoonishly evil advisor, a king who decides everything by shouting

**Specifics:**
- a seating plan argued over for a week
- the right to sit in the queen's presence
- a lady-in-waiting who hears everything
- petitions read by a secretary first
- a marriage negotiated through three ambassadors
- a household office (Master of the Horse) worth more than land
- a fall from favour signalled by an unreturned greeting
- a progress through the provinces that ruins the hosts
- precedence disputes at a funeral
- a dowry paid in installments, the last one late

### Parliaments, elections and campaigns
**Wakes on:** parliament, senator, election, campaign, minister, politician, парламент, депутат, вибор, міністр, політик

Modern politics runs on coalitions, staff and timing: votes counted in advance, press cycles, donors, constituency work; the speech is the end of a long process.

**Avoid:** every politician a liar, one dramatic speech that changes everything

**Specifics:**
- a whip counting votes the night before
- a committee hearing nobody watches
- a staffer drafting the statement before the event happens
- a constituency surgery about potholes
- a leak timed for the Friday news cycle
- a donor dinner with a seating chart
- a coalition deal hinging on one ministry
- a campaign bus with bad coffee
- polling numbers read like weather
- a vote missed because of a train delay

### Bureaucracy and offices
**Wakes on:** bureaucra, paperwork, permit, office, clerk, stamp, бюрократ, довідк, печатк, дозвіл, чиновник

Bureaucracy is people following procedures: forms, stamps, queues, the clerk who can help or not. The drama is in exceptions, connections and deadlines.

**Avoid:** faceless evil bureaucrats

**Specifics:**
- a stamp that can only be given on Tuesdays
- a queue number called out of order
- a form that needs another form to be filled
- a clerk who quietly tells you the shortcut
- a document valid for three months that takes four to get
- a photocopy of a photocopy
- the right person on holiday
- a box of chocolates to say thank you

Organised crime is a business with family, loyalty and fear; most of it is logistics, money laundering and relationships with legitimate people, not shootouts.

## Tropes

### Vampires
**Wakes on:** vampire, vampir, blood, undead, вампір, кров

Vampire lore varies by story: what sunlight does, invitations, reflections, how turning works, whether they need to sleep. Pick the rules this story uses and keep them; the best vampire stories are about time, hunger and what they lost.

**Avoid:** brooding in every scene, 'the hunger' as the only feeling, a velvet-and-candles castle by default

**Specifics:**
- old habits from their century (cash only, letter-writing)
- a careful relationship with a blood supplier
- a home with no mirrors and very good locks
- slowness about the modern world, or obsessive keeping up
- the need to be invited, played as etiquette
- outliving every friend
- a feeding that's clinical, not sensual
- a sire who still writes once a decade
- real fear of fire more than of stakes
- a day-sleeper's blackout curtains held with tape

### Werewolves and shapeshifters
**Wakes on:** werewolf, shifter, lycan, pack, full moon, перевертн, вовкулак, зграя

Shifter stories run on body and community: how changing feels, what the pack expects, control and its loss. Decide whether the moon forces it.

**Avoid:** 'alpha' as a personality, growling instead of talking

**Specifics:**
- clothes stashed in the woods for afterwards
- an appetite nobody at the office understands
- a pack meeting in someone's kitchen
- scars that healed too fast to explain
- a full moon schedule on the fridge
- a sense of smell that makes cities exhausting
- an elder who remembers the old territory lines
- torn trainers, always

### The fae and fairy courts
**Wakes on:** fae, faerie, fairy, seelie, unseelie, fey, фе, фейр, ельф

Fae are bound by rules, not morality: they cannot lie but twist the truth; names, thanks, gifts and iron matter; bargains are literal. Their courts are beautiful and dangerous.

**Avoid:** pretty elves with no rules, 'mischievous' as their only trait

**Specifics:**
- never saying 'thank you' because it implies a debt
- food in Faerie that keeps you there
- a true name guarded like a password
- iron nails in a doorframe
- a bargain worded so the loophole is obvious only later
- time passing differently: a night that was seven years
- salt lines on a windowsill
- a gift that cannot be refused politely

### Dragons

Dragons work best with a clear ecology and mind: how they fly, eat, think, what a hoard means to them, how long they live and remember.

**Specifics:**

### Omegaverse 🔞
**Wakes on:** omegaverse, a/b/o, abo, omega, alpha, beta, heat, rut, омегавер, омег, альф

Omegaverse is a fandom worldbuilding with a secondary dynamic (alpha, beta, omega) and its own biology: scent, heats and ruts, nesting, bonding marks, suppressants. Stories differ a lot in how society treats it; decide whether this world is hierarchical, modern and regulated, or somewhere between, and keep it consistent.

**Avoid:** alphas as only dominant and omegas as only submissive, everything reduced to scent and possessiveness

**Specifics:**
- scent blockers and suppressants with side effects
- a scent described specifically (rain on asphalt, bitter orange), not just 'sweet'
- a nest built from borrowed sweaters
- heat leave written into labour law
- a bonding mark that is a legal matter
- a beta who's tired of being overlooked
- scent-neutral public spaces
- a doctor's note about cycle timing

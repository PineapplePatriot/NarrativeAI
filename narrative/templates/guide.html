How to try Narrative AI

A roleplay app: you chat with characters, and the app keeps the story consistent, with presets, worldbooks, trackers, summaries and a setup assistant (a potato called Bulba). Five minutes is enough to see the main ideas.

The 5-minute tour

  1. Open a chat. On the home page, pick a character (the demo account has Il Dottore from Genshin Impact, with his own lore, mood pictures, background and music) and write what you do or say. Replies appear word by word.
  2. Try another version. The arrows under the AI's last reply make a new version (a "swipe"); the old one is kept, so you can flip between them.
  3. Steer it. Press the pen ✎ next to the message box. Type a rough wish in Director's note ("she dodges the question"), press 🥔 Word it for me to have Bulba phrase it, then ↻ Rewrite last reply.
  4. Ask Bulba why. If replies feel off (too long, too nice, writing your actions), press 🥔 Bulba in the chat's header and say so in plain words. Bulba looks at the chat, the preset and the character, explains the cause and proposes one fix, which you can preview, apply and undo.
  5. See the story state. The ▤ button above the chat opens the trackers: where and when the scene is, who's there, relationships, and so on.

Thinking models (MiMo, Claude) show what they thought before replying in a folded Thoughts box above the reply. Handy when a reply surprises you.

Bulba, the setup potato

Most roleplay apps hand you a wall of settings. Bulba asks what you like instead, shows you short samples written by your own model, and builds the setup from your answers.

  • Setup (🥔 Bulba in the top bar): extras, how replies should read (with A/B samples), your preset, who you are in the story, and the character. It can import a character card you already have, research a character or world from fandom wikis, write a worldbook for it, pick a background, music and dialogue colour, and suggest story extras that fit. At the end it sums up what was set up and where to change it, with downloads of your preset, card and worldbook (they work in SillyTavern too). The side panel shows which step you're on and roughly how many questions are left, and Skip this step skips anything (each skipped step is one less thing tuned to you).
  • Extras in one form: the full setup starts with one form for summaries, trackers, pictures, Bulba Watch, who fixes the habits you approve, and the background model, and the story extras (dice, letters, the ideas button) come in a second form with Bulba's picks ticked. Quicker, and cheaper than asking one by one. After the preset, Bulba asks once about formatting you always fix by hand (text rules), and at the end it lists what's waiting for you in the chat (director's notes, Condense, What the AI saw, the microphone).
  • Fast route: in a hurry? Pick a feel (back-and-forth, rich scene or director seat), Bulba applies the tested ready setup for your model, then only asks who you are in the story and who you want to talk to. Or skip Bulba entirely: after you pick your model, the welcome page offers the same ready presets as they are.
  • In a chat (🥔 Bulba in the chat header): fixes what bugs you in that chat. It prefers tested wording from well-known community presets over writing its own.
  • It can also fine-tune: model settings like reply length, which trackers a character has, alternate greetings, and making the other mood pictures from one.
  • Story systems (ask Bulba): rules the app keeps so the story can't rush or forget them. A slow meter such as Trust moves one step at most per scene and then holds for a few replies (and can fade back if neglected); a story clock moves with what happens; events arrive when the clock reaches them ("Day 3, 20:00: the masquerade"); an opt-in random table lets chance decide what you asked it to. Only scenes you keep count: a swiped, rewritten or edited reply moves nothing, and deleting messages rewinds. They show at the top of the ▤ panel, cost nothing extra (they ride on the tracker update) and Bulba can explain them in a chat ("why is he still cold?"). You can also set them up by hand on the character's tracker page (⚙ in the panel).
  • Bulba Watch (on by default): after every reply, free checks catch sentences that keep starting the same way and phrases repeated word for word; every few replies, Bulba also reads the latest ones for habits that keep repeating, such as the same sentence shapes, over-explaining, big wise lines a moment hasn't earned, or a character acting out of character. It learns what you like from the replies you rewrite or edit, and notices when you take much longer than usual to reply. The same read catches a reply getting the past wrong (retelling events inaccurately, leaving out what mattered, inventing details), and new facts worth keeping in the card or worldbook (Bulba proposes them; you Apply). One slip is nothing; when something keeps happening, the 🥔 Bulba button gets a number and a small note offers to look at it together. I don't mind this stops that note for good; Fix this automatically turns on Bulba's polish for it: every new reply, swipe or continued reply gets a quick pass that rewrites just the sentences with that habit (Bulba's model by default, or your chat model; a small extra call each time), with Show original and Undo fixes under the reply ("Write my message" gets the same polish). You can also ask Bulba in a chat to fix one message ("fewer metaphors in that reply"): Apply puts the fixed version in the chat. On the Extras page you can see what it learned about your taste (reword or remove any line), bring back habits you muted, or switch it off; one chat can switch it off from its first note.
  • Every change is a proposal with Apply and Undo. Nothing changes until you apply it.
  • Transcript downloads the whole conversation with Bulba (never any keys), for reporting odd behaviour.

The chat page

Messages

  • Hover or tap a message for edit, 🗜 (condense it: the AI gets a short version, you still see all of it; edit, redo or undo under the message), branch (a new chat that starts from that message) and delete.
  • 💬 Chats in the header lists all chats with this character, including branches.
  • Atmosphere: with the World tracker on, the scene shows the time of day (night, dusk, dawn) and the weather (rain, a storm, snow, fog) from it, for free; switch it off in the pen menu → This chat. The music's ▶/❚❚ sits in the header; in the music picker, Let the music follow the scene switches between the built-in tracks as the mood changes (one tiny call every few replies). 🎤 in the message box lets you speak your message.
  • The small button inside the message box, Write my message, has the AI write your next message (or flesh out your draft) in your voice. Nothing is sent until you press send.

The pen menu ✎

  • Director's note: steers the next reply. ▶ Send sends it on its own (the story goes on without a message from you) or with the message you wrote; Word it for me turns a rough idea into a clear note; Be creative has Bulba write two: what would naturally happen, and a twist. The note becomes a 🎬 message of its own in the chat: edit or delete it like any message, and Regenerate keeps it in mind. The AI always reads it as a direction, never as something said in the story.
  • Redo the last reply: shortcuts (area, thoughts, skip time, talk, shorter) or your own words, then Rewrite last reply.
  • Pinned note: something the AI should keep in mind for the whole chat.
  • Story memory: the summary (for long chats), the trackers, which lore entries the last reply used, and What the AI saw: everything the last reply got besides the preset and the messages. In long chats, recall brings back up to three older messages that had fallen out of the AI's context when the scene mentions them again (rare words only; free). Bulba in the chat sees the same and can fix a summary piece or the pinned note (Apply/Undo).
  • Actions: continue the last reply, spellcheck your message.
  • This chat: who you are in this chat, the dialogue colour, the layout (chat bubbles, or a book where replies read as chapters and your messages fold away), and this month's spending.
  • When you steer more than you play (director's notes, or messages Bulba Watch reads as directions), or switch to the book layout, Bulba offers director mode once per chat: one click, with Undo.

Extras (on the home page)

All optional, all off until you choose them:

  • Story summary and story trackers: automatic every few messages, or on request.
  • Character sprites: pictures that match the mood of each reply (made with Nano Banana from one picture).
  • Dice and inventory: for game-like stories, the app rolls real dice and keeps your items, so the AI can't make them up. Off means no luck in the story at all.
  • Story extras: when the scene calls for it, the app draws a letter, a phone screen, a noticeboard with rumours, a relationship milestone or a scrapbook keepsake right inside the reply. Suggested actions add buttons under each reply for when you're stuck.
  • Bulba's ideas: a 🥔 button next to the message box for when you're stuck: three ways to go on (what you could say or do, or, when you direct the story, what could happen next).
  • Background jobs: summaries and trackers can run on a cheaper model than your chat.

Presets, worldbooks and the rest

  • Presets: the instructions sent with every reply. Starters are tuned per model; SillyTavern presets import as they are, with their text rules (regex). The Preview tab shows exactly what's sent.
  • Worldbooks (SillyTavern calls them lorebooks): facts that enter the story only when they come up.
  • Characters: create your own, or import SillyTavern / Chub cards (.png or .json), with their lore, notes and text rules.
  • Samplers and Connections: model settings and API keys, for people who want them.

Costs

Every reply is paid on the connected OpenRouter key. The pen menu shows this month's total, and the app stops at the monthly limit ($25). Bulba shows what it has spent in its side panel.

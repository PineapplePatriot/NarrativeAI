How to try Narrative AI

A roleplay app: you chat with characters, and the app keeps the story consistent, with presets, lorebooks, trackers, summaries and a setup assistant (a potato called Bulba). Five minutes is enough to see the main ideas.

The 5-minute tour

  1. Open a chat. On the home page, pick a character (the demo account has Il Dottore from Genshin Impact, with his own lore, mood pictures, background and music) and write what you do or say. Replies appear word by word.
  2. Try another version. The arrows under the AI's last reply make a new version (a "swipe"); the old one is kept, so you can flip between them.
  3. Steer it. Press the pen ✎ next to the message box. Type a rough wish in Director's note ("she dodges the question"), press 🥔 Word it for me to have Bulba phrase it, then ↻ Rewrite last reply.
  4. Ask Bulba why. If replies feel off (too long, too nice, writing your actions), press 🥔 Bulba in the chat's header and say so in plain words. Bulba looks at the chat, the preset and the character, explains the cause and proposes one fix, which you can preview, apply and undo.
  5. See the story state. The ▤ button above the chat opens the trackers: where and when the scene is, who's there, relationships, and so on.

Thinking models (MiMo, Claude) show what they thought before replying in a folded Thoughts box above the reply. Handy when a reply surprises you.

Bulba, the setup potato

Most roleplay apps hand you a wall of settings. Bulba asks what you like instead, shows you short samples written by your own model, and builds the setup from your answers.

  • Setup (🥔 Bulba in the top bar): extras, how replies should read (with A/B samples), your preset, who you are in the story, and the character. It can import a character card you already have, research a character or world from fandom wikis, write a lorebook for it, pick a background, music and dialogue colour, and suggest story extras that fit. At the end it sums up what was set up and where to change it, with downloads of your preset, card and lorebook (they work in SillyTavern too). The side panel shows which step you're on and roughly how many questions are left, and Skip this step skips anything (each skipped step is one less thing tuned to you).
  • Fast route: in a hurry? Pick a feel (back-and-forth, rich scene or director seat), Bulba applies the tested ready setup for your model, then only asks who you are in the story and who you want to talk to. Or skip Bulba entirely: after you pick your model, the welcome page offers the same ready presets as they are.
  • In a chat (🥔 Bulba in the chat header): fixes what bugs you in that chat. It prefers tested wording from well-known community presets over writing its own.
  • It can also fine-tune: model settings like reply length, which trackers a character has, alternate greetings, and making the other mood pictures from one.
  • Bulba Watch (on by default): after every reply, free checks catch sentences that keep starting the same way and phrases repeated word for word; every few replies, Bulba also reads the latest ones for habits that keep repeating, such as the same sentence shapes, over-explaining, big wise lines a moment hasn't earned, or a character acting out of character. It learns what you like from the replies you rewrite or edit, and notices when you take much longer than usual to reply. One slip is nothing; when something keeps happening, the 🥔 Bulba button gets a number and a small note offers to look at it together. I don't mind this stops that note for good. On the Extras page you can see what it learned about your taste (reword or remove any line), bring back habits you muted, or switch it off; one chat can switch it off from its first note.
  • Every change is a proposal with Apply and Undo. Nothing changes until you apply it.
  • Transcript downloads the whole conversation with Bulba (never any keys), for reporting odd behaviour.

The chat page

Messages

  • Hover or tap a message for edit, branch (a new chat that starts from that message) and delete.
  • 💬 Chats in the header lists all chats with this character, including branches.
  • The small button inside the message box, Write my message, has the AI write your next message (or flesh out your draft) in your voice. Nothing is sent until you press send.

The pen menu ✎

  • Director's note: for the next reply only.
  • Pinned note: something the AI should keep in mind for the whole chat.
  • Story memory: the summary (for long chats), the trackers and which lore entries the last reply used.
  • Actions: continue the last reply, spellcheck your message.
  • This chat: who you are in this chat, the dialogue colour, the layout (chat bubbles, or a book where replies read as chapters and your messages fold away), and this month's spending.

Extras (on the home page)

All optional, all off until you choose them:

  • Story summary and story trackers: automatic every few messages, or on request.
  • Character sprites: pictures that match the mood of each reply (made with Nano Banana from one picture).
  • Dice and inventory: for game-like stories, the app rolls real dice and keeps your items, so the AI can't make them up. Off means no luck in the story at all.
  • Story extras: when the scene calls for it, the app draws a letter, a phone screen, a noticeboard with rumours, a relationship milestone or a scrapbook keepsake right inside the reply. Suggested actions add buttons under each reply for when you're stuck.
  • Bulba's ideas: a 🥔 button next to the message box for when you're stuck: three ways to go on (what you could say or do, or, when you direct the story, what could happen next).
  • Background jobs: summaries and trackers can run on a cheaper model than your chat.

Presets, lorebooks and the rest

  • Presets: the instructions sent with every reply. Starters are tuned per model; SillyTavern presets import as they are, with their text rules (regex). The Preview tab shows exactly what's sent.
  • Worldbooks (lorebooks): facts that enter the story only when they come up.
  • Characters: create your own, or import SillyTavern / Chub cards (.png or .json), with their lore, notes and text rules.
  • Samplers and Connections: model settings and API keys, for people who want them.

Costs

Every reply is paid on the connected OpenRouter key. The pen menu shows this month's total, and the app stops at the monthly limit ($25). Bulba shows what it has spent in its side panel.
